# J5. The citizen view

[Back to the manual](README.md)

> **In short:** Anyone can see what each published entity received, promised and delivered, in five languages, on
> a phone, with no account.

**Who this is for:** any member of the public who wants to know what a sport, arts or culture body
was given and what it delivered. No account is needed.

**What you will do:** find an organisation, open its record, go on to its own website, and read it
all in your language.

Only organisations the Department has chosen to publish appear here.

---

## 1. Open the list

Go to the Vuka address followed by `/public`, or follow a link someone shared.

![Citizen view on a phone: the search box and the three groups](img/j5-01-index.png)

- **Search for an organisation** by name, and tap **Search**.
- Or tap a group: **Sport**, **Arts** or **Culture** (heritage, libraries and language bodies are
  under Culture). The number beside each is how many published organisations it holds.

Under **Published organisations** a line counts them, the public funds allocated to them this year,
and how many of their targets were achieved.

## 2. Open an organisation's record

Each card shows the organisation's group, its allocation for the year, the last quarter it reported
with the Department's approval, and how many of its targets it has achieved. Tap **View
organisation** (1).

![Artscape's card with View organisation marked](img/j5-02-card.png)

The record follows the money in order, under **The accountability chain**: **01. Received** (the
funds allocated), **02. Promised** (the targets it committed to this year) and **03. Delivered**
(targets with a result in an approved report). Each figure names the document it comes from.

![Artscape's public record: the accountability chain](img/j5-03-record.png)

Further down are the funding detail, the audit outcomes published by the Auditor-General under
**Accountability history**, and **Where did this information come from?**. Figures are as reported
by the organisation and reviewed by DSAC, and published with the Department's approval.

## 3. Go to the organisation itself

Under **Useful links** is **Visit <organisation name>** (1), which opens the organisation's own
website in a new tab.

![Useful links with Visit Artscape marked](img/j5-03b-visit.png)

Vuka holds one thing about a funded body: what it was given and what it reported against it. Once
you have read that, the next questions (what is on this month, how to visit, who runs it) are
answered on the organisation's own site, not here. The link is there so you do not have to go
looking.

Two things to know:

- It is the organisation's website, not a government page. DSAC does not control what is published
  there, and the page says so under the link.
- Where no address is on record, there is no link. A wrong link under a government masthead would
  send you somewhere that is not the organisation at all, so Vuka would rather show nothing.

## 4. Read it in your language

Choose **English**, **Afrikaans**, **isiZulu**, **isiXhosa** or **Sesotho** from the language picker
at the top of every page.

![The citizen view in isiZulu](img/j5-04-isizulu.png)

A link you copy keeps the language, so the person you send it to opens the same page in the same
language.

## 5. Save data with the light version

On a slow connection or a small data bundle, tap **Light version, uses less data** at the bottom of
the page. It shows the same organisations and figures as plain text, with no pictures. Vuka also
chooses the light version by itself when your phone asks to save data or reports a slow
connection. Tap **Full version, with charts and search** to go back.

![The light version: each organisation on one line](img/j5-05-lite.png)

Pages you have already opened stay readable with no signal, marked with when they were saved.
