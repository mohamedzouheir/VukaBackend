# 4. The executive

[Back to the manual](README.md)

> **In short:** The executive sees all 28 entities on one page, opens any one to see what it was
> given, what it promised and what it delivered, can explain its risk score, follows the trends over
> the years, and asks Karabo questions in ordinary words. Nothing on these screens changes a figure.

**Signs in as:** DSAC executive (Director-General).

**The journey:** read the portfolio, open Boxing South Africa, explain its score, check its unit
cost, look at the trends in Analytics, and ask Karabo.

---

## Step 1. The portfolio

After signing in you land on **Portfolio**, for Q1.

![The portfolio with the allocation (1), the risk bands (2) and Ask Karabo (3) marked](img/j4-01-portfolio.png)

1. **The totals** across the top: R2.258bn allocated this year, 28 funded bodies, how many submitted
   Q1, targets reported against targets registered, submissions outstanding, and critical entities.
2. **By risk band**: every entity grouped by band, with its score and its allocation. The sector
   buttons above narrow it to arts, heritage, libraries, sport or language.
3. **Ask Karabo**, for step 6.

The allocations are the published figures from the Estimates of National Expenditure 2026, Vote 37,
Table 37.3.

## Step 2. Open Boxing South Africa

Click **BoxingSA** in the medium band (1).

![The medium band with Boxing SA marked](img/j4-02-worst.png)

The entity page follows the money from left to right: what it was **allocated**, what it
**promised** (targets in its Annual Performance Plan), what it **reported**, and how much of that is
**verified** with evidence. Each figure names the document it comes from. Under it are the
allocation year by year and the Auditor-General's audit history.

![Boxing South Africa: allocated, promised, reported, verified; the allocation by year; the audit history](img/j4-03-drilldown.png)

## Step 3. Explain the score

Scroll to the two buttons under **Outstanding**. Click **Explain the score** (1).

![Explain the score (1) and Unit cost (2) marked](img/j4-04-actions.png)

It is the same five-signal panel the reviewer sees, so the Department always gives one account of
the same fact.

![Boxing South Africa's score explained: five signals, totalling 32.5](img/j4-05-explain.png)

Close it and click **Unit cost** (2).

## Step 4. Unit cost

Pick an indicator. Vuka compares what it costs to deliver one unit three ways, always showing both
sides of each sum so anyone can check it:

- **Against its own plan**: planned spend over planned units, beside actual spend over actual units.
- **Against its own history**, where there is a record.
- **Against sector peers**: only other entities in the same sector.

![Unit cost for Boxing South Africa against its plan, its history and its sector peers](img/j4-06-unit-cost.png)

Vuka never compares cost across sectors. A library book and a boxing licence are not the same kind
of thing, so there is no screen that pretends they are.

## Step 5. Is the portfolio getting better?

Click **Analytics & Insights** in the menu. Four headline measures across the top, then the
portfolio drawn as charts: money and delivery year on year, audit outcomes, who filed on time, each
sector, and which entities moved up or down.

![Analytics & Insights with Ask Karabo (1), Download as CSV (2) and Print or save as PDF (3) marked](img/j4-08-analytics.png)

- A year the Auditor-General has not yet audited draws **no bar**, never a bar of zero.
- **Show the figures** beside each chart opens the exact table it was drawn from.
- **Download as CSV** (2) and **Print or save as PDF** (3) take it to a committee meeting.

## Step 6. Ask Karabo

Click **Ask Karabo** (1). Karabo opens beside the page. Click a suggested question, or type one.
Here: *Why is Robben Island scored critical?*

![Karabo: Robben Island is scored 45, medium, with the five signals and the source](img/k-05-exec-risk.png)

Karabo corrects the question: Robben Island is scored 45, medium, not critical. It lists the signals
behind the score and, under **Where this came from**, the record it read. It answers only from
Vuka's records, with the executive's own access, and says so when the record does not hold the
answer.

Ask a follow-up in the same conversation: *How is Iziko doing this quarter?*

![Karabo summarising Iziko's quarter, with its sources](img/k-06-exec-iziko.png)

The executive's journey ends here.
